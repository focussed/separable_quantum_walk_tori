\title{Complete Classification of Full State Revivals on Separable Quantum Walks on Tori}\author{Ian Craig}\email[Contact author: ]{ian.craig@atu.ie}\affiliation{Atlantic Technological University, Sligo, Ireland}\author{Michael McGetterick}\affiliation{University of Galway, Galway, Ireland}\date{\today}
\begin{abstract}A quantum walk revives when it returns to its exact starting state
after some number of steps. In this paper, we verify the work of
Dukes on 1D cycles and extend it to toroidal lattices to find and classify
all exact revivals in the 2D case. To achieve this, we use a separable coin
parameterised by reflection parameters $\rho_x$, $\rho_y$ and phase parameters
$\delta_x$, $\delta_y$.
In 2D, the separable walk factorises as a tensor
product of two 1D walks. A torus revives when both axes revive.
It can also revive when the two axes' phases cancel each other,
at a period shorter than their least common multiple.
The 2D classification contains revivals in
$28$ tori, $59$ different periods, and $18{,}174$
distinct parameter sets.
On the 1D cycle, we establish within the search bound that exactly seven
cycle lengths admit an exact revival in one dimension,
$k \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ and verify the positive cases at the operator level.
We run an operator-level search on a
$1001 \times 360$ grid over $(\rho, \delta)$, for $k \le 16$ and
$N \le 60$, and find no exact revival for the remaining cycle lengths.
We find one correction to Dukes' 1D tables: the $k = 2$, $\rho = 1/2$,
$\delta \in \{\pi/2, 3\pi/2\}$ exact revivals at $N = 24$
lie in Dukes' seed family but were not tabulated, because Dukes' original
seeding was too constrained to reach them.  We offer a
conjecture, supported by a cyclotomic-field argument, that no cycle
length other than $\{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ admits an exact revival.

\end{abstract}

\maketitle\section{\label{sec:intro}Introduction}\par
Periodicity is a fundamental aspect of physics and a clock is an
essential component of most computer systems. An analogous phenomenon
occurs in the quantum realm: under certain conditions on symmetric
topologies, a quantum walker returns to its initial state, both
position and spin, after some number of steps. We call this a
\emph{full-state revival}. Such revivals have been proposed as a
resource for quantum memory \cite{Chandrashekar2015} and for quantum
state routing \cite{Steinfurth2025}, as a system that returns
periodically to a previous state can serve as a clock, a register, or
a routing primitive.

Here we consider a discrete-time quantum walk (DTQW) with a separable
four-dimensional coin. For a DTQW, $U$ is the single-step operator of
the walk. A full-state revival at period $N$ means $U^N = I$: the
operator is the identity, so every state returns to itself with no
phase after $N$ steps. A related phenomenon also occurs along the
walk: the state may return to itself up to an unobservable global
phase, $U^N = e^{i\phi} I$ for some $\phi \neq 0$. We call this a
\emph{projective} revival. The classification in this paper is of
full-state revivals in the strict sense, $U^N = I$; the projective
revivals are noted where they occur but are not the object of
classification.
\par
Full-state revivals are rare. They occur only for certain coins on
symmetric topologies, and their spectral identity is equally special:
for $U^N = I$ to hold, every eigenphase $\theta_j$ of the walk operator
$U$ must be a rational multiple of $2\pi$, $\theta_j = 2\pi m_j / N$
for integer $m_j$ and a common period $N$. In other words, the
eigenvalues of $U$ must all be roots of unity, sharing a common power
$N$ that returns the walk to its initial state, $U^N = I$
\par
Dukes \cite{Dukes2014} classified full-state revivals on 1D cycle
graphs. Building on this work, we extend the classification to
separable DTQWs over 2D toroidal lattices. A
separable coin factorises into independent 1D coins on each axis of a
$k \times m$ torus. The two factors revive independently, and the
torus walk revives when they both do. The classification covers the admissible 
cycle lengths $k, m \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ with revival periods up
to $N = 420$.
\par
Dukes' coin is parameterised by a real reflection parameter $\rho$
and a phase $\delta$. The 2D coin has the corresponding
parameters $\{\rho_x, \delta_x, \rho_y, \delta_y\}$. We report the
classification as a set of records
$(k, m, N, \rho_x, \delta_x, \rho_y, \delta_y)$, each record giving a
torus, an exact revival period, and the coin parameters. The 
classification records each distinct parameter set at its smallest
exact revival period.
\par
Previous work on 2D lattices involved separable coins.   \v{S}tefa\v{n}\'ak et al.\
\cite{Stefanak2010} stated that the four-state walk
on a 2D lattice admits $N = 2$ revivals but no
longer cycles within the class considered.
Later Dodangodage \cite{Dodangodage2018} constructed walks with
revivals of longer period and Jayakody \cite{Jayakody2018} showed that a non-separable coin can
be designed to produce revivals of any desired period.  In that construction the period is a free
parameter: the coin and shift are built so that the walk returns after any chosen number of steps.
Our classification covers the separable case, where the (parameterised) coin and shift are fixed 
to the standard forms on each axis and the period is not chosen but emerges from the walk itself.
We consider the two results to be complementary. Jayakody answers whether a revival of any given period
can be built.  More recently, Steinfurth et al. \cite{Steinfurth2026} demonstrated revivals experimentally using coupled optical-fibre loops.  By changing the coin over time in a way that respects a simple mirror symmetry, their scheme can produce a revival at almost any chosen period for any input state, because the coin is modulated rather than fixed.
\par
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:theory} defines the separable walk and its
four-parameter coin, gives a worked single-step example, and
states the classification theorems. Section~\ref{sec:method}
describes how the classification was computed, and
Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the results.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the results and the
classification, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarises
the output, states the open problem and the outlook.
\section{\label{sec:theory}Theoretical Framework}\subsection{Separable coin walk on a Torus}\par
The single-step evolution of the walker on a DTQW is governed by the 
unitary operation
\par
\begin{equation}
U = S \, (I_p \otimes C),
\label{eq:general_evolution}
\end{equation}
where \(I_p\) denotes the identity on the position space, \(C\) is the
coin operator acting on the internal degree of freedom, and \(S\) is
the conditional shift operator that entangles position and coin by
displacing the walker according to its coin state.
\par
In the 1D cycle setting, Dukes \cite{Dukes2014} introduced the
parameterised coin operator
\par
\begin{equation}
C(\rho, \delta) =
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt{\rho} & \sqrt{1-\rho}\, e^{i\delta} \\[4pt]
\sqrt{1-\rho}\, e^{-i\delta} & -\sqrt{\rho}
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:1d_coin}
\end{equation}
where \(\rho \in [0, 1]\) physically dictates the spatial splitting
ratio of the walker as it transitions to adjacent nodes, while
\(\delta \in [0, 2\pi)\) governs the relative phase accumulated between
these divergent paths. We note that 
\(\delta\) does not enter the trace of the single-step evolution
operator for the 1D walk and consequently, the eigenphase
spectrum---and thus the revival period---depends solely on \(\rho\).
The phase parameter \(\delta\) merely redirects the walker along
different directional paths without altering the revival periodicity.
Setting \(\rho = \frac{1}{2}\) and \(\delta = 0\)
creates the Hadamard coin:
\par
\begin{equation}
C\left(\frac{1}{2}, 0\right) =
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\[4pt]
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{pmatrix} \equiv H.
\label{eq:hadamard_coin}
\end{equation}
\par
The 1D construction extends to a two-dimensional $k \times m$
toroidal lattice. The total state space is assembled as a tensor
product of position and coin Hilbert spaces,
$\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_p \otimes \mathcal{H}_c$. The spatial
component $\mathcal{H}_p = \mathcal{H}_{p_x} \otimes
\mathcal{H}_{p_y}$ is spanned by the basis
$\{|x, y\rangle : x \in \mathbb{Z}_k, y \in \mathbb{Z}_m\}$, with
periodic boundaries along both axes. The coin space
$\mathcal{H}_c = \mathcal{H}_{c_x} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{c_y}
\cong \mathbb{C}^4$ carries the directional basis
$\{|L\rangle, |R\rangle\} \otimes \{|D\rangle, |U\rangle\}$, with
$L, R$ denoting displacements along the $x$ axis and $D, U$ along
the $y$ axis. The ordered basis is:
\par
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
|LD\rangle, \quad |LU\rangle, \quad |RD\rangle, \quad |RU\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\par
A separable coin factorises as \(C_{k \times m} = C_x(\rho_x,
\delta_x) \otimes C_y(\rho_y, \delta_y)\), with independent 1D
parameters on each axis. In the ordered basis above, the matrix representation is
\par
\begin{widetext}
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
C_{k \times m} = \\[2pt]
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt{\rho_x \rho_y} & \sqrt{\rho_x (1-\rho_y)}\, e^{i\delta_y} & \sqrt{(1-\rho_x)\rho_y}\, e^{i\delta_x} & \sqrt{(1-\rho_x)(1-\rho_y)}\, e^{i(\delta_x+\delta_y)} \\[8pt]
\sqrt{\rho_x (1-\rho_y)}\, e^{-i\delta_y} & -\sqrt{\rho_x \rho_y} & \sqrt{(1-\rho_x)(1-\rho_y)}\, e^{i(\delta_x-\delta_y)} & -\sqrt{(1-\rho_x)\rho_y}\, e^{i\delta_x} \\[8pt]
\sqrt{(1-\rho_x)\rho_y}\, e^{-i\delta_x} & \sqrt{(1-\rho_x)(1-\rho_y)}\, e^{-i(\delta_x-\delta_y)} & -\sqrt{\rho_x \rho_y} & -\sqrt{\rho_x (1-\rho_y)}\, e^{i\delta_y} \\[8pt]
\sqrt{(1-\rho_x)(1-\rho_y)}\, e^{-i(\delta_x+\delta_y)} & -\sqrt{(1-\rho_x)\rho_y}\, e^{-i\delta_x} & -\sqrt{\rho_x (1-\rho_y)}\, e^{-i\delta_y} & \sqrt{\rho_x \rho_y}
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:full_4x4_coin_matrix}
\end{equation}
\end{widetext}
\par
Similar to the 1D case, the trace diagonal of this matrix contains only
expressions in $\rho_x$ and $\rho_y$, so the revival period depends
only on these two parameters.

As the spatial axes are orthogonal and the coin acts separably,
the conditional shift is $S = S_x S_y$. With subsystem identities
$I_x \equiv I_{p_x} \otimes I_{c_x}$ and
$I_y \equiv I_{p_y} \otimes I_{c_y}$, the two components are
\par
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_x = \sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}_k} \Big( &|x-1\rangle\langle x| \otimes |L\rangle\langle L| \\
+ &|x+1\rangle\langle x| \otimes |R\rangle\langle R| \Big) \otimes I_y,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shift_x}
\end{equation}
\par
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_y = I_x \otimes \sum_{y \in \mathbb{Z}_m} \Big( &|y-1\rangle\langle y| \otimes |D\rangle\langle D| \\
+ &|y+1\rangle\langle y| \otimes |U\rangle\langle U| \Big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shift_y}
\end{equation}
where all index shifts are understood modulo \(k\) and \(m\),
respectively (the toroidal boundary conditions).
\par
The separability of the coin and the orthogonality of the axes yield
a tensor-product factorisation of the full unitary. Reordering the
total Hilbert space as $\mathcal{H} = (\mathcal{H}_{p_x} \otimes
\mathcal{H}_{c_x}) \otimes (\mathcal{H}_{p_y} \otimes
\mathcal{H}_{c_y})$, the single-step operator factorises as
\par
\begin{equation}
U_{k \times m} = U_k \otimes U_m,
\label{eq:factorised_unitary}
\end{equation}
\par
where $U_k = S_x (I_k \otimes C_x)$ and $U_m = S_y (I_m \otimes C_y)$
are independent 1D evolution operators on the $x$- and $y$-cycles.
The factorisation isolates the spectral constraints along each axis,
so the root-of-unity conditions that govern full-state revivals can
be evaluated dimension-by-dimension.

A torus revival requires the sum of the two axis phases to be zero. When both axes revive 
at a common step, the sum vanishes at the LCM of the two axis periods; but two nonzero 
phases of opposite sign can also sum to zero at a step where neither axis has revived on its 
own, which gives a torus revival at a period shorter than the LCM of the two axis periods.

\emph{Note on exact versus projective revivals.}

In all cases a revival requires both the position and spin state of the walker to
return to its initial values.  In this work we distinguish between two classes of revivals:
a \emph{projective} revival is one in which the state returns up to an
unobservable global phase ($U^N = e^{i\phi} I$).  An \emph{exact}
or full-state revival is one where the state returns with no phase ($U^N = I$). 
Here we enumerate only exact revivals. 
\subsection{The Mechanics of a Separable Walk on a Torus}\par
To give an example of the separable walk, we evaluate a single step
on a $3 \times 3$ torus with $\rho_x = \rho_y = 2/3$ and
$\delta_x = \delta_y = 0$. The walker begins at the origin in coin
state $|RU\rangle = |R\rangle \otimes |U\rangle$. The single-axis coin
operator is
\begin{equation}
C\!\left(\tfrac{2}{3}, 0\right)
= \begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt{2/3} & \sqrt{1/3} \\
\sqrt{1/3} & -\sqrt{2/3}
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:coin-2-3}
\end{equation}
acting on the basis $\{|\text{negative}\rangle, |\text{positive}\rangle\}$
along each axis. Its action on the initial state is
\begin{align}
C_x |R\rangle &= \sqrt{1/3}\,|L\rangle - \sqrt{2/3}\,|R\rangle, \\
C_y |U\rangle &= \sqrt{1/3}\,|D\rangle - \sqrt{2/3}\,|U\rangle,
\end{align}
so that the tensor product $C_x \otimes C_y$ produces the post-coin
superposition
\begin{equation}
(C_x \otimes C_y)|RU\rangle
= \tfrac{1}{3}|LD\rangle - \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}|LU\rangle
- \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}|RD\rangle + \tfrac{2}{3}|RU\rangle.
\label{eq:post-coin}
\end{equation}

The conditional shift $S = S_x S_y$ then displaces each branch by one
site in the direction indicated by the coin state, mapping the four
basis states on the $3 \times 3$ torus as
\begin{align}
|LD\rangle &: (0,0) \to (2, 2), \\
|LU\rangle &: (0,0) \to (2, 1), \\
|RD\rangle &: (0,0) \to (1, 2), \\
|RU\rangle &: (0,0) \to (1, 1).
\end{align}
The resulting state
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
|\psi_1\rangle
={}& \tfrac{1}{3}|2,2\rangle|LD\rangle
- \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}|2,1\rangle|LU\rangle \\
&- \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}|1,2\rangle|RD\rangle
+ \tfrac{2}{3}|1,1\rangle|RU\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:state-after-one-step}
\end{equation}
is entangled in position$\otimes$coin, with the norm preserved:
\begin{equation}
\langle\psi_1|\psi_1\rangle
= \tfrac{1}{9} + \tfrac{2}{9} + \tfrac{2}{9} + \tfrac{4}{9} = 1.
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:one-step} shows the splitting of amplitude across the
four directions and the subsequent distribution of the branches to
their destination nodes.

Two features of this step are worth noting. First, position and coin
are entangled after a single shift, reflecting the correlation between
the two axes that the separable coin introduces. Second, unlike the
$\rho = 1/2$ Hadamard case, the split is asymmetric: the $|RU\rangle$
branch retains amplitude $2/3$, the two off-diagonal branches carry
$-\sqrt{2}/3$, and the opposite-corner branch carries $1/3$.
\par

\subsection{Classification Theorems}\par
Having established revivals on 1D cycles for \(k \in \{8,10\}\), one
might intuitively anticipate that larger cycles---such as
\(k \in \{12,16,\dots\}\)---would similarly support full-state revivals
with relative ease. Yet, as we shall demonstrate, this expectation
proves incorrect: the algebraic constraints imposed by the dispersion
relation become increasingly rigid as the cycle size grows.
\par
\begin{theorem}[1D existence]
\label{thm:1d-existence}
A discrete-time quantum walk on a 1D cycle graph
$\mathbb{Z}_k$ driven by Dukes' coin $C(\rho, \delta) \in
\mathrm{U}(2)$ admits a non-trivial full-state revival for
every $k \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$.
\end{theorem}
\par
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:1d-existence}]
For $k \in \{2, 3, 4, 6\}$ Dukes gives families of solutions
\cite{Dukes2014}, \S4.2--4.4; for $k \in \{5, 8, 10\}$ he gives finite
sets of explicit triples $(\rho, \delta, N)$ in \S4.5. Each triple is
a witness to the existence of a revival at that $k$. Substituting each
witness into the dispersion relation
$\sin\theta_j = \sqrt{\rho}\sin(2\pi j/k)$ and checking that every
eigenphase is a rational multiple of $2\pi/N$ verifies
$U_k^N = I_{2k}$ directly.
\end{proof}
\par
\begin{theorem}[1D non-existence within the search bound]
\label{thm:1d-nonexistence}
An exhaustive search over a grid of $\rho \in \{j \cdot
10^{-4} : j = 1, \ldots, 9999\}$ and $\delta/\pi$ at $1/180$
spacing (360 base points), augmented by the Dukes-exact
$\delta$ values, for revival step count $N \le \Nmax$, finds no
non-trivial full-state revival for any $k \notin \{2, 3, 4,
5, 6, 8, 10\}$ with $k \le \Kmax$. The completeness of the
1D classification for $k \ge 17$ and $N > \Nmax$ remains
open (see Conjecture~\ref{conj:no-other-k}).
\end{theorem}
\par
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:1d-nonexistence}]
We performed a grid search over the parameter space
$\rho \in \{j \cdot 10^{-4} : j = 1, \ldots, 9999\}$,
$\delta/\pi$ at $1/180$ spacing (360 base points) augmented by
the Dukes-exact $\delta$ values, and $N \in \{1,
\ldots, \Nmax\}$, for each $k \in \{2, \ldots, \Kmax\}$.
For every candidate, we evaluated the root-of-unity residual
$r_N(\rho, \delta) = \max_j |\theta_j - m_j \cdot 2\pi/N|$.
Grid points with residual below $10^{-3}$ were refined by
bounded scalar minimisation followed by a local sweep, and
accepted only if the residual after refinement fell below
$10^{-10}$ and the independent matrix-exponentiation check
$\|U_k^N - I_{2k}\| < 10^{-10}$ also passed. The search
found no revival for any $k \in \{7, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16\}$.
The search is reproducible from the EXP032b notebook in the
accompanying repository \cite{full_revivals_references}.
\end{proof}
\par
\begin{corollary}[2D existence]
\label{cor:2d-existence}
A separable quantum walk on a $k \times m$ toroidal lattice
exhibits a full-state revival whenever both spatial
dimensions belong to the 1D revival set $k, m \in \{2, 3,
4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ and their revival period sets share a
common element, $\mathcal{N}_k \cap \mathcal{N}_m \neq
\emptyset$.
\end{corollary}
\par
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:2d-existence}]
The 2D evolution operator factorises as $U_{k \times m} =
U_k \otimes U_m$ following the tensor-product decomposition
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:factorised_unitary}. If $k, m \in \{2, 3,
4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:1d-existence} supplies
1D witnesses. If $\mathcal{N}_k \cap \mathcal{N}_m \neq
\emptyset$, choose a common period $N$. Then
$U_{k \times m}^N = U_k^N \otimes U_m^N = e^{i(\phi_x +
\phi_y)} I_{4km}$, the stated revival.
\end{proof}
\par
\begin{corollary}[2D non-existence within the search bound]
\label{cor:2d-nonexistence}
For $k, m \le \Kmax$, no $k \times m$ toroidal lattice
outside the class of Corollary~\ref{cor:2d-existence}
exhibits a full-state revival at any period $N \le \Nmax$.
\end{corollary}
\par
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:2d-nonexistence}]
Suppose a separable walk on a $k \times m$ torus admits a
full-state revival at step $N$. By the factorisation
$U_{k \times m} = U_k \otimes U_m$, this is equivalent
to $U_k^N = e^{i\phi_x} I_{2k}$ and $U_m^N = e^{i\phi_y}
I_{2m}$ simultaneously. By Theorem~\ref{thm:1d-nonexistence},
no such $N \le \Nmax$  exists when $k \notin \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$
and $k \le \Kmax$; the same holds for $m$. If both $k$
and $m$ are admissible but $\mathcal{N}_k \cap
\mathcal{N}_m = \emptyset$, no common step count exists.
\end{proof}
\par
\begin{conjecture}[no revivals outside the admissible set]
\label{conj:no-other-k}
No cycle length $k \notin \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ admits a
non-trivial 1D full-state revival at any period $N$ or any coin
parameter $\rho \in (0, 1)$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:no-other-k}]
The dispersion relation for a $k$-cycle is
$\sin\theta_j = \sqrt{\rho}\,\sin(2\pi j/k)$ for
$j = 1, \ldots, k-1$. A revival at period $N$ forces each
eigenphase to satisfy $\theta_j = \pi r_j / N$ for some integer
$r_j$. Taking the ratio of the dispersion relations for the two
lowest spatial modes ($j = 2$ and $j = 1$) eliminates $\rho$
entirely:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sin\theta_2}{\sin\theta_1}
= \frac{\sin(4\pi / k)}{\sin(2\pi / k)}
= 2\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right).
\label{eq:mode-ratio}
\end{equation}
The coin parameter $\sqrt{\rho}$ cancels out, leaving a purely
geometric constant on the right-hand side. Because
$\theta_1, \theta_2 \in \pi\mathbb{Q}$, that constant must equal
a ratio of sines of rational angles. For $k = 2$ the cycle has
only one spatial harmonic ($j = 1$), and the revival condition
is trivially solvable by choice of $\rho$.

For $k \ge 3$ the constant $2\cos(2\pi/k)$ generates the real
cyclotomic subfield $\mathbb{Q}(\cos(2\pi/k))$, whose degree over
$\mathbb{Q}$ is $\phi(k)/2$, where $\phi$ is Euler's totient
function \cite{Washington1997}. The degree of this field
therefore controls whether a single real parameter $\rho$ can
synchronise the harmonics. For $k \in \{3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ 
the degree is at most two, and
the constant is rational or quadratic; for $k = 12$ it is two; for
$k \in \{7, 9\}$ and $k \ge 11$ it is at least three.

The case $k = 12$ also has $\phi(12)/2 = 2$, but the cycle
carries three non-trivial harmonics ($j = 1, 2, 3$). The third
gives $\sin\theta_3 = \sqrt{\rho}\,\sin(\pi/2) = \sqrt{\rho}$.
Niven's theorem restricts the sine of a rational angle to the
values $0, \pm\tfrac{1}{2}, \pm 1$ \cite{Niven1956}, so
$\sqrt{\rho}$ must lie in that set. Alignment with the $j = 1$
and $j = 2$ harmonics then forces $\theta_3 = \pi/2$ uniquely,
hence $\rho = 1$. The coin reduces to a deterministic reflector,
and no non-trivial revival exists in the open interval
$\rho \in (0,1)$.

For $k \in \{7, 9\}$ and all $k \ge 11$ except $k = 12$, the
degree satisfies $\phi(k)/2 \ge 3$. The minimal polynomial of
$2\cos(2\pi/k)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ then has degree at least
three. If a revival existed, applying the automorphisms of
$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})/\mathbb{Q})$ to the
dispersion relations would map the trigonometric algebraic
integers to their Galois conjugates, generating at least three
independent constraints on the single parameter $\rho$. Whether
these constraints are in fact independent is the open step. If
they are, the system revives at only the trivial, boundary solutions
$\rho \in \{0, 1\}$.

We therefore state the conclusion as a conjecture, supported on
the search bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:1d-nonexistence} and
consistent with the explicit witnesses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:1d-existence}.
\end{remark}
\section{\label{sec:method}Methodology}\par
The classification is built and verified in two notebooks,
\texttt{EXP033.ipynb} and \texttt{EXP032b.ipynb}, available in the
accompanying repository \cite{full_revivals_references}. The method
proceeds in four stages.

\emph{Revival demonstration (EXP033).} We evolve the walk step by step for
$k = 4$, $\rho = 1/2$, $\delta = \pi/2$ and record the state at each
step. The walker returns to its initial position and spin at
$N \in \{6,12,18,24\}$; these four revivals carry different global
phases, three projective and one exact. This demonstrates the
distinction between the two criteria on a single worked example.

\emph{1D verification (EXP033).} We transcribe the complete set of 1D revival
solutions tabulated by Dukes for cycle lengths
$k \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$, together with the $k = 2$,
$\rho = 1/2$, $\delta \in \{\pi/2, 3\pi/2\}$ correction, and verify
each entry at the operator level using the eigenvalue residual
\begin{equation}
r(k, \rho, \delta, N)
= \max_j \left| \lambda_j^N - \overline{\lambda^N} \right| ,
\label{eq:eig-residual}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_j$ are the eigenvalues of $U_k(\rho, \delta)$ and
$\overline{\lambda^N}$ is their $N$-th power mean. The residual
vanishes when $U^N = e^{i\phi} I$; the exact case $U^N = I$ is
confirmed separately by a matrix-power check at the recorded period.

\emph{Exhaustive grid search (EXP032b).} We search for exact
revivals on 1D cycles for $k \le 16$ on a $1001 \times 360$
grid over $(\rho, \delta/\pi)$, with $\rho$ at $10^{-3}$
spacing and $\delta/\pi$ at $1/180$ spacing, augmented by the
Dukes-exact $\delta$ values, and test $U^N = I$ up to $N = 60$
at each grid point. The operator is diagonalised once per grid
point, and the eigenvalue residual $\max_j |\lambda_j^N - 1|$
is evaluated for every $N$. Grid points that pass the tolerance
are recorded as \emph{candidate revivals}. Each candidate is
then refined: its parameters are snapped to the nearest
algebraic value, Snapping moves the point by at most $10^{-3}$ in $\rho$ and
$10^{-3}$ in $\delta/\pi$, and the snapped point must then pass
a stricter check than the detection threshold: $10^{-12}$ on the
eigenvalue residual and $10^{-10}$ on the matrix-power residual,
against the $10^{-9}$ used for detection. Candidates that fail
refinement are reported as \texttt{unconfirmed} and treated as a
bug rather than a result.  the exact period is confirmed by a direct
matrix-power check $\|U^N - I\| < 10^{-10}$, and the confirmed
revival is classified as trivial, Dukes-tabulated, or new.
Trivial revivals are those at the degenerate parameter values
$\rho \in \{0, 1\}$ or $\delta \in \{0, \pi\}$.

\emph{2D enumeration (EXP033).} For a separable walk on a $k \times m$ torus,
the single-step operator factorises as
$U_{k \times m} = U_k \otimes U_m$. We enumerate all ordered pairs
$(k, m)$ with $k, m \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ and $k \le m$,
every pair of 1D signatures on those cycles, and every $N$ from $1$
to $420$. For each pair, the operator is diagonalised once and the
projective residual is evaluated at every $N$; the exact period is
derived from the phase order via
$N_{\text{exact}} = N_{\text{proj}} \cdot \mathrm{ord}(\phi)$,
where $\mathrm{ord}(\phi)$ is the smallest $r$ with $\phi^r = 1$.
Scanning every $N$ rather than only multiples of the factor LCM is
necessary because the phase-cancellation case can revive exactly at
a proper divisor of the LCM. Each canonical record is confirmed by a
matrix-power check $\|U^N - I\| < 10^{-10}$ at its exact period.

\section{\label{sec:discussion}Discussion}\par
\subsection{2D Classification}
The results of the tensor factorisation show that:
$28$ tori revive, at $59$ distinct
periods, with $18{,}174$ distinct parameter sets in total.
The distribution is highly uneven. The largest families
occur on the tori $4 \times 4$, $3 \times 4$, and
$3 \times 3$, which together account for just under half the
classification. These are the cycles with the densest 1D
spectra: the $3$-cycle carries 41 signatures and the
$4$-cycle 69, so their tensor products
generate many distinct parameter combinations. The tori
involving $k = 2$ are the sparsest, because the $2$-cycle
carries only three signatures, at $N_{\min} \in \{2, 24, 24\}$.

Note the 1D grid search stops at $N \le 60$, while the 2D
classification extends to $N \le 420$. 
The 2D enumeration is built from the catalogued 1D
signatures, whose periods are all at most $60$, and a 2D period is
either one of those periods or a combination of a pair of them.
The 2D scan therefore inherits the 1D catalogue rather than
re-searching 1D at larger $N$; periods above $60$ arise only as
combinations of 1D revival periods.
\par
The period spectrum is also scattered. Of the $59$ distinct
periods, the majority are populated by only a handful of
parameter sets. The peak periods are $N = 60$ with $2{,}456$
distinct parameter sets, $N = 120$ with $1{,}642$, $N = 420$ with
$1{,}076$, $N = 24$ with $719$, and $N = 30$ with $581$. Each is
highly composite and hence an LCM of many pairs of 1D revival
periods, so a large number of 2D combinations share it. At the
other extreme, the periods $N = 2$ and $N = 6$ are isolated on
one torus and on three tori respectively.
\par
The reflection ($\rho_x, \rho_y$) parameters take several algebraic forms. The
low-lying signatures are rational ($\nicefrac{1}{2}$,
$\nicefrac{1}{3}$, $\nicefrac{1}{4}$, $\nicefrac{2}{3}$,
$\nicefrac{3}{4}$) or quadratic surds of the form
$(a \pm \sqrt{b})/c$ with $b \in \{2, 3, 5\}$. The surds
split into three families: the $\sqrt{2}$-family, associated
with the $4$-cycle; the $\sqrt{3}$-family, associated with the
$3$- and $6$-cycles; and the pentagonal family
$(5 \pm \sqrt{5})/10$, associated with the $5$- and
$10$-cycles. The higher-period signatures at $N \ge 14$ on
the $3$- and $4$-cycles are trigonometric rather than
quadratic --- for example the $k=3$ value $0.251007$ at
$N=14$ and the $k=4$ value $0.188255$ at $N=14$ --- but they
remain algebraic and their minimal polynomials are determined
by the corresponding root-of-unity angle. The duality
$\rho \leftrightarrow 1 - \rho$ holds without exception
(due to the definition of the coin), so
each reflection parameter is paired with its complement
elsewhere in the classification.
\par
The phase ($\delta_x, \delta_y$) parameters are also
a notable feature of the 2D
classification. In the 1D case, $\delta$ affects only the
eigenvectors of $U_k$; the eigenvalues, and hence the revival
period, depend solely on $\rho$. This simplicity does not
survive the tensor product. In 2D, the eigenvalues of
$U_{k \times m}$ are products $\lambda_i \mu_j$, and a
period-$N$ revival requires the phases of the two factors to
be compatible. The phase parameters $\delta_x$ and $\delta_y$
control which pairs of 1D eigenbases can be aligned. As
Table~\ref{tab:delta-summary} shows, only $25.9\%$ of the
 classification has $\delta_x = \delta_y = 0$, while
$48.9\%$ has zero phase on exactly one axis, $5.0\%$ has
$\delta_x = \delta_y \neq 0$, and the remaining $20.2\%$
requires unequal non-zero phases on the two axes. All
appearing phases are rational multiples of $\pi$ whose
denominators divide twice the cycle length. The phase
parameters therefore act as a selection mechanism: the great
majority of 2D revivals require a non-zero phase on at least
one axis.
\par
\subsection{The $10 \times 10$ revival: a case study}
The $10 \times 10$ torus is the largest torus admitting the
period $N = 60$, and provides a clean illustration
of the revival mechanism. We take $\rho_x = \rho_y =
(5 - \sqrt{5})/10 \approx 0.2764$ and $\delta_x =
\delta_y = 0$.

\section{\label{sec:conclusion}Conclusion}\par
We have presented a complete classification of full-state
revivals for separable quantum walks on $k \times m$
toroidal lattices, within the search bounds $k, m \in
\{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10\}$ and $N \le 420$. The 1D case is
exhaustive on the search grid: exactly seven cycle lengths
admit revivals, and the operator-level verification of all
172 signatures is reported in Table~\ref{tab:1d-catalogue-exact}
in the Appendix.  The 2D case
factorises via the tensor product, giving $28$ tori, $59$
periods, and $18{,}174$ distinct parameter sets. We demonstrated
an example of exact versus projective revivals in 1D, reported
a single correction to Dukes' 1D tables --- the $k = 2$,
$\rho = 1/2$, $\delta \in \{\pi/2, 3\pi/2\}$ exact revival at
$N = 24$, which lies in Dukes' seed family but was not
tabulated --- and stated a conjecture that the 1D admissible set
is complete beyond the search bound, sketching a
cyclotomic-field approach to the proof.

Summarising the 2D classification, two features stand out.
First, the phase parameters $\delta_x$ and $\delta_y$ are a
selection mechanism in 2D: only $25.9\%$ of the distinct
classification has $\delta_x = \delta_y = 0$, $48.9\%$ has
zero phase on exactly one axis, $5.0\%$ has
$\delta_x = \delta_y \neq 0$, and the remaining $20.2\%$ has
unequal non-zero phases on the two axes; thus $74.1\%$ of the
classification requires a non-zero phase on at least one axis.
Second, the period
spectrum is dominated by the highly composite periods: $N = 120$
occurs on $22$ tori, $N = 60$ on $25$, and $N = 24$ on $9$,
while the sparsest periods ($N = 2$, $N = 42$) reach only one
and three tori respectively. These results open directions for
quantum state routing and quantum memory architectures, and
invite a proof of the stated conjecture.
